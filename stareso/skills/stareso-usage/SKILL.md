# stareso-usage

**Session:** standard tier, any session (cheap: one tool run, then the report). About 5 turns. Talk in English unless the user switches to French. This step proposes no workflow change: that is `/stareso-retro`'s job, and only when the user asks for changes.

## 1. Refresh and compute
1. `node <stareso>/tools/session-stats.mjs --log` (refreshes `docs/data/sessions.jsonl`).
2. `node <stareso>/tools/usage-report.mjs` (add `--all` when the user asked for every milestone). It prints six tables: step types, sessions, milestones (the 30 most recent), steps that merged slices, efficiency by tier, planning rounds. `--json` gives the same numbers as one compact JSON line, for questions the tables do not answer. Copy its numbers: never recompute or count by hand.

## 2. Report (in this order)
1. **First line: what ≈ equiv is**, in one sentence: one number per session that weighs the token kinds by their relative cost (cache read × 0.1, cache write × 1.25, output × 5), frozen weights, not money.
2. **The tables** as the tool printed them. Share % is the first column. Work done before the workflow started (before the first init or retrofit) is shown with "—" and left out of the shares. Sessions are named by number and date, never by their step. When milestones are hidden, say how many and offer `--all` in one line.
3. **Charts**, when the harness can draw them (the visual capability): `node <stareso>/tools/usage-report.mjs --charts` prints every series ready to paste (tiles, share by step type, cumulative cost, cost per slice by milestone, context per turn by session, tokens against lines by tier, tier medians, outliers). Draw the six charts of the section "The charts" below, in that order, and never reshape a number in the chat. Default layout: compact, four tiles then the charts paired in two columns (1 beside 6, then 2 and 3 full width, then 4 beside 5), about 200 px high each, legends under the charts. On request ("detailed", "tall", or one chart by its number) draw the tall layout: one full-width chart at a time with its guide lines, tier medians, outlier ring and the stacked conductor chart with one row per step. Without a visual capability, the tables alone. The same charts, tables and recent builds open in any browser with `node <stareso>/tools/dashboard.mjs --write` (a standalone page, data inlined, written to the temp folder: give its path).
4. **Reading:** three to five lines. The biggest cost of the period and its cause in the records, how chat-built slices compare with launched builds (≈ equiv per slice), and which tier gives the most change per token. Compare with the previous usage report or retro entry when there is one.

## 3. Record
Nothing is written by default. When the user wants to keep the report, write it as a dated page in the project, `node <stareso>/tools/dashboard.mjs --write docs/reports/usage-yyyy-mm-dd.html` (standalone, data inlined, no local path, Chart.js from its CDN), and commit docs.

## How the numbers are built (for questions)
- **≈ equiv per slice** of a milestone: the ≈ equiv of the step that merged each slice (session and helpers together) ÷ the slices that step merged, so a slice built in a chat and one built by a launched build compare directly. "Measured" says how many merges had a logged step.
- **Efficiency by tier:** helper tokens from the build records, lines changed by the slice's merge commit outside `docs/` (kept in the build record when slice-done merged it). Tokens per 100 lines is the median per tier, and a slice above twice its tier's median token count is an outlier. Tiers are chosen by difficulty, so compare a slice with its own tier, not across tiers.
- **Planning rounds** are named `Test n` / `Improve n` in date order, with their session number and the share of their cost spent re-reading older context (Carried).

## The charts (what each one shows, how to read it: answer questions from here)
Four tiles come first: the workflow cost (≈ equiv since the first init or retrofit), the cost before the workflow (shown, never in a share), the slices built, and the last launched build's cost per slice.

1. **Share of the workflow cost by step type** (horizontal bars). One bar per step type (build, test, improve, chat, status, retro), as a percentage of the workflow cost. Reading: where the tokens go. Build is expected to lead; a large "chat" bar means work done outside the steps. The tooltip gives ≈ equiv, active hours and turns.
2. **Cumulative cost by step type over time** (stepped lines, log scale, x axis in dates). Each line is the running total of one step type; a vertical jump is one step's whole cost, placed at the step's start. The log scale keeps the small types readable beside build. Reading: when each type started costing and which steps made the jumps (a chat line that climbs steeply is a chat that built or tested). Variant: stacked areas on a linear scale show the composition of the total instead of the timing.
3. **Cost per slice by milestone** (bars coloured by "built by": launched build, in chat, mixed; a dashed reference line at project A M5, 335k). The value is the cost of the step that merged the slices (session and helpers together) divided by the slices it merged, so chat-built and helper-built milestones compare directly. No bar means the session that built it left no transcript. Reading: launched builds should sit near the reference, chat-built milestones sit far above it; a milestone whose slices were merged during a test carries that test's cost too, which is a limit of the measure, not a cost of the slices.
4. **Context per turn by session** (line; point size = turns; red = over 300 turns). Context per turn = the session's cache read ÷ its turns: the mean of what each turn re-read from the cache (every assistant turn reports it), not the context size at the end, and a little under the true context since fresh input and cache writes are not in it. Reading: it grows with the length of a session and with what the session carried from earlier steps; launched sessions sit near the base (about 100k), long mixed sessions climb past 200k. A red point is a session that should have been split.
5. **Helper tokens against lines changed, one point per slice** (scatter, log scales, colour and shape = tier; crosses = tier medians; grey diagonals = constant tokens per 100 lines; red ring = outlier, over twice its tier's median tokens). Reading: each tier forms its own cloud (light bottom left, strong top right), because tiers are chosen by difficulty: compare a point with its own cloud, never across tiers. A point far above its cloud cost more than its size justifies (look at its note with `usage-report.mjs --builds`); a point far below is a cheap large change. The diagonals let the eye read tokens per line; the medians per tier are the table's "tokens per 100 lines".

6. **Conductor against helpers, per step that merged slices** (stacked horizontal bars: the session's own cost and its helpers' cost in ≈ equiv; the label gives the slices merged). The conductor share is `metrics.md`'s "conductor vs helpers" (target: conductor under 50 % of a build). Reading: a launched build should be mostly helpers; a bar that is all conductor is a chat or a test that built or merged itself; a build with a large conductor part spent its turns on checks, rebuilds or fixes rather than delegating. Helper cost is the helpers' own ≈ equiv, cache writes included (before the datasets it left the cache writes out, so older reports show a larger conductor share).

A session marked "(transcript gone)" (`session-stats --print`) was logged, then its transcript was deleted (archiving or clearing a session in the app deletes it): its numbers come from `docs/data/sessions.jsonl`, to which `session-stats --log` appends every session that changed, so a session logged once never drops out of the record again. A session archived before any `--log` kept it can be brought back from an older USAGE.md in git with `node <stareso>/tools/usage-seed.mjs <git-rev> <session-number>` (its entry says "seeded"); the report's "helper-built slices have no ≈ equiv per slice" line tells when that is worth doing. A slice the build records say a helper built (`docs/data/builds.jsonl`, or BUILD-LOG in a project not migrated yet) is charged to a build step only: with none in the records it is unlogged, never charged to a test or chat step that was open at the time.

Costs are ≈ equiv unless the chart says tokens (helper tokens come from the build records and are plain token counts).
